Stripe IPO readiness analysis

Overview

Stripe is not a payments processor. It is financial infrastructure. Its APIs are embedded in the core payment flows of Amazon, Ford, and OpenAI, and migrating means rewriting financial plumbing, not cancelling a SaaS subscription. That switching cost is the foundation of the investment thesis.

The objective was to determine a fair enterprise value for Stripe ahead of its anticipated IPO in late 2026 or H1 2027, and to assess whether the $159B private valuation is a compelling entry point. The output is a formal investment memorandum with a blended three-method valuation and a BUY recommendation.

Methodology

The valuation blends three methods, weighted to reflect what the market will actually pay at IPO rather than intrinsic value alone. Comparable company analysis carries 60% weight because it answers the most relevant pre-IPO question: what does the market pay today for 26%+ growth infrastructure?

The DCF spans 10 years of explicit free cash flow forecasts, anchored to Stripe's 2025A revenue of $7.6B and a 0.40% net take rate applied to $1.9T in total payment volume. WACC is 11.66%, built from a 4.44% risk-free rate (US 10Y), a beta of 1.30x, and Damodaran's 5.55% equity risk premium. Terminal growth is 3.0%.

Seven peers across three cohorts were analysed: payments infrastructure (Adyen, Block, Payoneer), payment networks as moat benchmarks (Visa, Mastercard), and e-commerce platforms (Shopify, PayPal). The 18x EV/Revenue multiple reflects Stripe's Rule of 40 score of 58%, ahead of Shopify at 47.3% and approaching Adyen at 67.3%.

Key findings

  • Blended EV of $174.9B implies +9.98% upside to the $159B private valuation, so risk/reward is asymmetrically positive ahead of IPO.
  • Comps (60% weight) imply $175.1B at 18x 2026E revenue. The DCF (10%) yields a $56.0B floor, and the IPO range (30%) anchors at $213.9B at 22x.
  • FCF grows from $1.57B (2025A) to $7.44B (2034E), with EBITDA margin expanding from 30% to 35%+ as fixed-cost leverage scales.
  • The bear case ($125.6B) requires take-rate compression and macro deterioration at the same time, a low-probability joint scenario that supports the BUY.
  • AI agent commerce is an unmodelled free option: autonomous transactions need a programmable payments layer, and Stripe's API-first architecture is the default candidate.
